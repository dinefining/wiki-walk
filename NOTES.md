# Wiki Walk: maintainer notes

A pixel forest of Wikipedia articles at the intersection of nature, technology and human individuality, walked through together.

Every plant is an article. You walk at ground level: the screen shows only a few steps around you, so a river is something you come across, not a line on a map. Articles grow in three shapes: a **cross** for shorter articles, a **diamond** for long ones about one theme, and a hollow **ring** where the themes mix (more than one colour). Colours: pale yellow `#f4fc8c` nature, sky blue `#86e2fa` technology, pink `#eeaef4` being human.

There are no theme regions: groves of related articles and lone plants are scattered over the whole park, every theme everywhere, with a river, a creek, ponds, a lake and wilderness trails (with plank bridges) between them. (Older notes below may still mention three circular biomes; those are gone.) Three circular biomes overlap like a Venn diagram on the ground: **nature**, **technology** and **human individuality**. Articles that belong to two sets grow in the overlaps; articles that belong to all three stand in the central clearing. The land is made of chunky blocks: terraced ground, thickets of undergrowth around the groves, a river with sandy banks through the nature biome, two creeks, ponds, and grass tufts in the meadows.

An article's colour says what it's made of: **pine green** for nature, **blue spruce** for technology, **heather** for human individuality, mixed in quarters from its own Wikipedia categories. Flowers are paler, like blossoms; trees darker, like canopies.

Walkers can also **plant** one article a day: double-click (or long-press) open ground, type a Wikipedia title or paste a link, and a new plant grows there for everyone, coloured by its own categories and sized by its length. Planted articles carry a tiny dot off their top-right corner in their planter's tint, the same pale colour as that walker's cursor.

Small things make it cozy: water glints drift down the stream, lanterns flicker, pastel butterflies and dandelion fluff drift on the breeze by day, and a few fireflies wander around you at dusk and night.

The sound toggle sits in the top-right. A minimal strip in the bottom-right shows `visited/all(planted)`, for example `13/15,483(23)`: articles you've visited, all articles in the forest, and how many of those walkers planted. Then how many walkers are in the forest now and your local time. **info** in the top-left explains the forest.

**Sound** (off until you turn it on; your choice is remembered) is synthesized in the browser, with no audio files: footsteps that match the ground under you (grass, thicket, sand, shallow water), birdsong by day, crickets at night, leaves rustling in a drifting breeze, and the stream growing louder as you come near water.

There is no search, no zoom and no map. You only see what's around you. Other people walking at the same time pass by as small pale cursors. The only thing you can do is leave a lantern on an article you liked. The forest follows your local time: by day you see around you, at dusk it darkens in steps, and at night you see only a small bubble of light, other walkers' lights and the lanterns people left behind.

## Walking

| | |
|---|---|
| Scroll / trackpad | walk in any direction (Shift + wheel walks sideways); scroll harder to jog |
| Click-drag / touch-drag | walk; drag faster to jog |
| Arrow keys (or WASD) | walk; hold Shift to jog |
| Hover a plant | it brightens |
| Click a plant | open its panel |
| Enter | open the article nearest to you |
| L | leave a lantern (or take yours back) on the open article, the one under the mouse, or the nearest one |
| Click elsewhere / Esc | close the panel |
| Double-click / long-press open ground | plant an article there (one a day) |
| **T** | debug: cycle local time → day → dusk → night |

Your last position is remembered in `localStorage`. The first visit starts somewhere random.

## Run it locally

The app is static files with no build step. It needs to be served over HTTP (browsers won't `fetch` `world.json` from `file://`):

```bash
python3 -m http.server 8000
```

Then open <http://localhost:8000>.

## Going live, step by step

Two free services: **Supabase** lets walkers see each other and share lanterns and plantings; **GitHub Pages** hosts the files. About 20 minutes, no command line needed.

**1. Supabase (the shared part)**

1. Sign up at [supabase.com](https://supabase.com) and create a new project. Pick any name, a database password you store somewhere safe, and the region closest to most of your walkers. Wait a minute while it sets up.
2. In the left sidebar open **SQL Editor**, click **New query**, paste the whole of `supabase.sql` (the same SQL as in *Walking together* below, in one file), and click **Run**. You should see "Success. No rows returned".
3. Open **Project Settings**. Copy the **Project URL** (under *Data API*, or on the project's home page), then under **API Keys** copy the **anon public** key, a long key starting `eyJ` (on newer dashboards it's under the *Legacy API keys* tab). Use the anon key rather than the newer `sb_publishable_` key, which the Supabase library version used here may not accept. Never use the `service_role` / secret key in the browser.
4. Open `config.js` in a text editor and paste them in:

   ```js
   window.WIKIPARK_CONFIG = {
     SUPABASE_URL: 'https://abcdefgh.supabase.co',
     SUPABASE_ANON_KEY: 'eyJ…',
     FREE_SCROLL: false,
   };
   ```

   The key is meant to be public: the Row Level Security rules in the SQL are what protect the data.

**2. GitHub Pages (the website)**

1. Sign up at [github.com](https://github.com), click **+ → New repository**, name it (e.g. `wiki-walk`), make it **Public**, and create it.
2. On the new repository's page click **uploading an existing file** and drag in the seven files: `index.html`, `style.css`, `main.js`, `config.js` (with your keys), `world.json`, `prepare.js`, `README.md`. Click **Commit changes**.
3. Go to **Settings → Pages**. Under *Build and deployment* choose **Deploy from a branch**, branch **main**, folder **/ (root)**, and **Save**.
4. After a minute or two the page shows your address, like `https://your-name.github.io/wiki-walk/`. That's the park.

**3. Check it**

Open the address in two different browsers (or a phone and a laptop). Walk to the same spot: you should see each other's pale arrows, and the walker count in the corner should say 2. Leave a lantern in one; it appears in the other.

**Updating later**

Upload the changed files to the repository again (same *Add file → Upload files*). Before you do, bump the `?v=` tags in `index.html` and `VERSION` in `main.js` (any new text, e.g. today's date) so visitors' browsers fetch the new files. GitHub takes a minute to publish.

**Your own domain (optional)**

In **Settings → Pages → Custom domain**, enter e.g. `park.maybemachines.com`, then at your domain registrar add a `CNAME` record from `park` to `your-name.github.io`. Tick **Enforce HTTPS** once it's verified.

**Good to know**

- Supabase's free tier pauses a project after a week with no activity. Visits keep it awake; if it does pause, unpause it from the dashboard. Without Supabase the park still works, solo.
- The free tier allows 200 people walking at once, plenty for a park.
- Visitors are anonymous, so the daily planting limit is kept in their browser. If someone abuses planting or lanterns, you can delete rows in Supabase's **Table Editor**.

## Walking together (Supabase details)

Without configuration Wiki Walk runs in **solo mode**: you walk alone and your lanterns stay in your browser.

To share cursors and lanterns:

1. Create a project at [supabase.com](https://supabase.com).
2. In the SQL editor, run:

   ```sql
   create table public.lanterns (
     id bigint generated always as identity primary key,
     article_title text not null check (char_length(article_title) between 1 and 300),
     x integer not null check (x between 0 and 4096),
     y integer not null check (y between 0 and 4096),
     secret_hash text, -- sha-256 of a secret only the walker who left it knows
     created_at timestamptz not null default now()
   );

   create index lanterns_created_at on public.lanterns (created_at);

   alter table public.lanterns enable row level security;

   create policy "anyone can see lanterns"
     on public.lanterns for select to anon using (true);

   -- Clients can only add lanterns stamped "now"; they can't edit or delete.
   create policy "anyone can leave a lantern"
     on public.lanterns for insert to anon
     with check (created_at > now() - interval '1 minute' and created_at < now() + interval '1 minute');

   -- Let new lanterns appear for everyone in realtime.
   alter publication supabase_realtime add table public.lanterns;

   -- Taking a lantern back: only with the secret the walker's browser kept.
   create or replace function public.remove_lantern(lantern_id bigint, secret text)
   returns boolean language sql security definer set search_path = public as $$
     with d as (
       delete from lanterns
       where id = lantern_id
         and secret_hash = encode(sha256(convert_to(secret, 'UTF8')), 'hex')
       returning 1
     )
     select exists (select 1 from d);
   $$;
   grant execute on function public.remove_lantern(bigint, text) to anon;
   ```

   Already set up from an earlier version? Add the column, then create the function above:

   ```sql
   alter table public.lanterns add column if not exists secret_hash text;
   ```

   And for planted articles:

   ```sql
   create table public.plantings (
     id bigint generated always as identity primary key,
     article_title text not null unique check (char_length(article_title) between 1 and 300),
     x integer not null check (x between 0 and 4096), -- the plant's centre
     y integer not null check (y between 0 and 4096),
     n smallint not null check (n between 0 and 4), -- nature, in quarters
     t smallint not null check (t between 0 and 4), -- technology
     h smallint not null check (h between 0 and 4), -- human individuality
     size smallint not null default 0 check (size between 0 and 2), -- flower, bush, tree
     tint smallint not null default 0 check (tint between 0 and 7),
     created_at timestamptz not null default now(),
     unique (x, y),
     check (n + t + h = 4)
   );

   alter table public.plantings enable row level security;

   create policy "anyone can see plantings"
     on public.plantings for select to anon using (true);

   create policy "anyone can plant"
     on public.plantings for insert to anon
     with check (created_at > now() - interval '1 minute' and created_at < now() + interval '1 minute');

   alter publication supabase_realtime add table public.plantings;
   ```

3. Copy the project URL and the **anon public** key (Project Settings → API) into `config.js`:

   ```js
   window.WIKIPARK_CONFIG = {
     SUPABASE_URL: 'https://xxxx.supabase.co',
     SUPABASE_ANON_KEY: 'eyJ...',
   };
   ```

The anon key is meant to be public; Row Level Security above is what protects the table.

How it works:

- **Cursors** travel over a Realtime broadcast channel (`wikipark-walk`), throttled to about 10 updates a second while moving and a heartbeat every 2 seconds while still. They're positioned in world coordinates and drawn only when they fall inside your view, so someone passing slides in from one edge and out the other. Received positions are eased. Presence removes walkers who close the tab; walkers whose tab is hidden fade out after 12 seconds.
- **Lanterns** are rows in `lanterns`. Three a day per visitor (counted in their browser from the lanterns they left today; taking one back gives it back). You can take back your own lantern (L again, or the card's lantern icon): your browser keeps a secret for each lantern it left, the row stores only its hash, and `remove_lantern` deletes the row only when the secret matches, so nobody can remove anyone else's. Removals stream to everyone too. Lanterns from the last 30 days are loaded on arrival and new ones stream in. More lanterns on one article make its glow slightly larger; each lantern dims in steps over 30 days. Old rows are simply ignored; you can prune them now and then:

  ```sql
  delete from public.lanterns where created_at < now() - interval '30 days';
  ```

- **Plantings** are rows in `plantings`. They never fade. An article can only grow once in the forest (whether in `world.json` or planted), and only on open ground or thicket, never on water or sand. The planter's tint index is stored so their dot keeps its colour. Without Supabase, plantings stay in your browser.

The daily planting limit is enforced in the browser only, since visitors are anonymous. If someone abuses it, add rate limiting in front of the insert (for example an Edge Function or a trigger that caps inserts per minute).

## What may grow

Every plant has to be worth stopping for. An article grows in the forest (from `prepare.js`, and when a walker plants one) only if it is:

1. **a real article**: not a redirect, disambiguation or "may refer to" page, list, outline, glossary, index or timeline;
2. **substantial**: at least 4,000 bytes and not tagged as a stub;
3. **about a person or a company only if it bridges the themes**: its own categories have to place it in at least two of nature, technology and human individuality. Konrad Lorenz, Charles Darwin, Stelarc, Rodney Brooks, Vandana Shiva, Bernie Krause, Ecosia and Ars Electronica grow here; celebrities and companies that belong to one theme don't. Categories that only name-check a theme (record-label rosters, internet fame) don't count.

When the world was last built, 17,980 candidates were fetched and 12,855 passed, 100 of them bridging people and companies, which always make it in. Left out: 3,202 too short, 647 people and 320 companies outside the themes, 483 redirects, 459 stubs, 13 disambiguation pages, 1 list. 12,000 grow. The rules live in `whyNot` in `prepare.js` and `whyNotHere` in `main.js`; keep them in step.

## Recordings

Real recordings from Wikipedia and Wikimedia Commons, never pronunciations or spoken versions of articles:

- **Clicking an animal in the forest** (a plant whose article is about an animal, or one of the creatures) plays a short sample, about 2.5 seconds from the loudest part of its recording, faded in and out. Click again, hear it again. Samples play only with forest sounds on.
- **♪ beside a card's title** appears when the article has a recording at all: a bird's song, a whale, a theremin. It plays the whole recording; click again to stop. This is an explicit click, so it plays even with forest sounds off.

Recordings are found through the article's media list (`/api/rest_v1/page/media-list/`) and played from the MP3 version Wikimedia keeps of each Ogg file.

## Things to stumble on

Not mentioned in the forest itself; they're there to be found. The fixed ones are placed from a fixed seed (in `main.js`, `placeSecrets`), so they're in the same spots for everyone, and nobody can plant on top of them.

- **Three stone wells**, one per biome, drawing from Wikipedia's daily feed (`/api/rest_v1/feed/featured/`): the nature well shows the picture of the day, the technology well today's featured article, the human well three "on this day" events. They change every day.
- **A message in a bottle** sometimes drifts along water near you. Catch it (click) for the featured article of a random day since 2010.
- **Five fairy rings**: small toadstools in a circle around a bigger one that twinkles now and then. Click the middle one: the park dissolves in dark pixels and you reappear somewhere random, a nod to Wikipedia's Random article.
- **The ancient tree**, big and gnarled, alone in the central clearing: *Atlas Shrugged*, whose first surviving edit is from 28 January 2001, two weeks after Wikipedia began.
- **A night owl** sometimes perches on a tree or bush inside your light and hoots (a real tawny owl, with sound on). Click it for *Owl*.
- **A blackbird** sometimes lands on a tree near you by day and sings; **a frog** sits on a bank at dusk and night and calls. Click them for their articles.
- **A deer** may be grazing in a meadow by day or dusk. Seen from above it's a small brown shape about the size of a bush; walk within about 18 steps and it bolts away from you. Click it before it does for *Deer*.

## Rebuilding the world

`world.json` is already included. To regenerate it (Node 18+):

```bash
WIKIPARK_CONTACT=https://github.com/you/wiki-walk node prepare.js
```

Wikimedia rate-limits clients that don't identify themselves, so set `WIKIPARK_CONTACT` to your repository URL or an email address. The script makes about 2,000 requests one at a time, so a full fetch takes 25–35 minutes. Add `--fetch-only` to stop after fetching.

The fetched article list and categories are cached in `.wikipark-cache.json` and `.wikipark-categories.json`, so you can tweak the layout and rerun instantly. Use `node prepare.js --refresh` to fetch from Wikipedia again.

What it does:

1. Pulls articles from seed categories (and some of their subcategories) for each set:
   - **Nature**: Ecology, Biodiversity, Botany, Mycology, Rivers, Forests, Ethology, Pollination, Trees, Wetlands …
   - **Technology**: Computing, Robotics, Renewable energy, Internet culture, Sensors, Artificial intelligence, Human–computer interaction …
   - **Human individuality**: Identity, Perception, Memory, Crafts, Play, Emotions, Creativity, Personality, Senses …
2. Marks articles that turn up in more than one set, adds a curated list of known bridge articles (Biomimicry, Bioacoustics, Quantified self, Solarpunk, Biophilic design, Citizen science, Cyborg …) and runs a few searches that surface more of them.
3. Applies the rules in *What may grow* and keeps 12,000 of the articles that pass, then lays them out: three circles overlapping like a Venn diagram, each article in the region that matches its sets. Articles from the same source category cluster into groves with meadow between them; about one in eight grows alone out in the meadows, so long stretches are never completely empty.
4. Reads each article's own categories and scores them against nature, technology and human keywords to get its colour mix (stored in quarters).
5. Shapes the land on a 4px block grid: elevation terraces from value noise, a meandering stream and creek, ponds, sandy banks, and thickets that thicken around groves but leave each grove a clearing. Articles never land on water.

The layout uses a fixed random seed, so the same article list always produces the same forest. Edit the seed lists at the top of `prepare.js` to grow a different one.

`world.json` format:

```js
{
  "w": 2080, "h": 2080,
  "sets": ["nature", "technology", "human"],
  "circles": [[cx, cy, r], ...],          // one per set, in bit order
  "block": 4, "gw": 260, "gh": 260, "bands": 6,
  "terrain": "0123…",  // one base-36 char per 4px block: band + bands * feature
                        // features: 0 ground, 1 thicket, 2 deep thicket, 3 water, 4 ripple, 5 sand
  "sprites": [[".#.", "###", ".#."], ...],          // flower, bush, tree, seen from above
  "sizeLengths": [bush, tree],                      // article bytes from which a plant is a bush / a tree
  "articles": [[title, mask, x, y, n, t, h, size], ...] // x, y: the plant's centre
                                                         // mask: 1 nature, 2 technology, 4 human
                                                   // n + t + h = 4: colour mix in quarters
}
```

## Notes

- Rendering happens on a small canvas (about 75 world pixels across the short side of the screen, so one world pixel is 6 to 20 screen pixels) scaled up with `image-rendering: pixelated`. Only what's in view is drawn, in a single `requestAnimationFrame` loop that pauses while the tab is hidden.
- Dusk and dawn last about an hour and step through three palettes: golden hour, a heather dusk, blue twilight. Their times shift a little with the seasons (dusk roughly 17:30 in December, 20:00 in June).
- At night everything outside a light is one flat, almost-black green: ground, water and thickets alike. What a light reaches is lamp-lit, not daylight: warm, and dimmer in dithered steps away from the light. Light falls off in dithered pixel rings, never gradients: about 10 pixels around you, 5 around other walkers, and a warm halo around lanterns. Articles can only be seen and opened inside light.
- Other walkers' cursors are drawn on their own overlay where a pixel is always 3 screen pixels, so they stay cursor-sized however far the world is zoomed in.
- You move on foot. Scrolling, dragging and the keys say where you want to go, and you get there at a walking pace (26 world pixels a second). Push harder (fast scrolling or dragging, or Shift with the keys) and you jog at more than twice that, but only for about 4 seconds. Out of breath, you're slower than walking and can't jog again until you've mostly recovered (about 13 seconds walking, 8 standing still). To bring back the original direct scrolling, set `FREE_SCROLL: true` in `config.js`.
- The world ends in a wall of dense forest. You can see it and walk up to it, but not into it.
- A small triangle in the bottom-left points from you to the nearest lantern (hover it for roughly how many steps). It hides when there are no lanterns, and while the nearest one is already in view.
- To leave a lantern, light the lantern on the tab at a card's top-left edge, or press L. The × tab above it closes the card. Do it again to take yours back. The ↗ beside an article's title opens it on Wikipedia. It's dim when unlit, faintly warm when others have left one, and glows once you've left yours. By day a lantern shows as a small warm cross with twinkling sparks; at night it lights the plants around it.
- By day you see a wide circle around you. At its edge the world fades out in a few dithered steps into darkness, so you only see (and can only click) what's inside it. Other walkers' arrows show by day only inside your circle; at night they carry lights. A lantern warms the ground around it in three round, gently breathing rings.
- All corner boxes share one size (30px tall; the square ones 30×30), set by `--hud` in `style.css`.
- When you publish changes, bump the `?v=` tags in `index.html` and `VERSION` in `main.js` so browsers fetch the new files instead of cached ones.
- With `prefers-reduced-motion`, the view moves without easing or fling, and the glints, flicker, butterflies and fireflies hold still or stay away.
- Article summaries come from the Wikipedia REST API (`/api/rest_v1/page/summary/`). Article text is © Wikipedia contributors, CC BY-SA 4.0.
